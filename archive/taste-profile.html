The shape

You watch everything and rate almost nothing

Fewer than one in ten films gets a score, so the 30 ratings carry the whole profile. Everything else — the full Marvel run, all the Harry Potters, Minecraft Movie — is just what was on.

What the ratings keep circling is doubling. The Prestige has twins, Persona has two faces merging, Adaptation. has twin brothers. Being John Malkovich, The Double, Memento, Us, Companion, The Substance, Mickey 17. And underneath that, someone coming apart: Good Time, Joker, Hereditary, The King of Comedy, Saint Maud.

The correction from this round is that neither of those is sufficient on its own. Enemy is a man meeting his exact double and it got a shrug. Beau Is Afraid is three hours of unravelling and once was enough. The unravelling has to arrive somewhere. That was already in your own notes as "emotional payoff" and I under-weighted it.

Lineage

You've been collecting descendants without the ancestors

This is the clearest structural finding on the page. Almost every director you've completed sits at the end of a line, and you've skipped the person they were copying. Filling those in is the highest-yield move available, and it explains the Bergman anomaly too.

Bergman → modern horror
Ingmar Bergman — Persona, The Seventh Seal (both 5★)
Robert Eggers — The Lighthouse is a two-hander that dissolves into Persona
Ari Aster — cites Cries and Whispers as the model for Hereditary's grief
This isn't a coincidence. Your two favourite modern horror directors both descend directly from the one filmmaker you've given perfect scores to twice. Hour of the Wolf is the missing link — Bergman's own horror film, 90 minutes, and the thing Eggers was reading.
Cronenberg → Cronenberg
David Cronenberg — zero films logged
Brandon Cronenberg — Possessor, Infinity Pool (his son)
Coralie Fargeat — The Substance
The biggest hole in the whole profile. You have the son and not the father, and David Cronenberg is arguably the most on-thesis director you've never watched. Dead Ringers (1988) is about identical twin doctors losing track of which is which — the purest version of the doubling idea that runs through everything you rate highly. Then Videodrome and The Fly.
Lumet & Cassavetes → the Safdies
Sidney Lumet — Dog Day Afternoon (1975)
John Cassavetes — the handheld, sweaty, actor-first tradition
The Safdies — Heaven Knows What, Good Time, Uncut Gems, Marty Supreme (4/4)
You've finished the Safdies and haven't touched the film Good Time is built out of. Dog Day Afternoon is 125 minutes, set over one sweltering day, and moves faster than most things released this year. It's the strongest argument that "old" was never the real variable.
Mann & Roeg → Nolan
Michael Mann — Heat (1995). Zero Mann logged
Nicolas Roeg — Don't Look Now (1973), where the fractured editing comes from
Christopher Nolan — 11 films, your most-watched director
Nolan has said outright that Heat was the direct model for The Dark Knight, and that Roeg's cutting shaped Memento. Don't Look Now is doubly relevant — it's also a grief-horror film about a doubled figure, and it's the other ancestor of Hereditary.
Buñuel → Lanthimos → a Korean original
Luis Buñuel — The Exterminating Angel, the direct model for Dogtooth
Yorgos Lanthimos — 5 films, Poor Things and Bugonia both 4½
Bugonia is a remake. Save the Green Planet! (2003, Jang Joon-hwan) is the Korean original, and you rated the copy 4½ without seeing it.
Polanski → Aster
Roman Polanski — Repulsion logged, Rosemary's Baby not
Ari Aster — Hereditary 4½
Hereditary is a Rosemary's Baby structure: a family conspiracy the protagonist is the last to understand. You already have the other half of Polanski's apartment trilogy.
Carruth → Benson & Moorhead
Shane Carruth — Primer (2004), Upstream Color (2013)
Benson & Moorhead — all five films
You swept an obscure indie sci-fi duo and skipped the two films that made their whole mode possible. Primer is 77 minutes and cost $7,000.

Worth restating the case for Stalker honestly: it's still 161 minutes of men walking and talking, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But Villeneuve names it as the reason Arrival and Dune look the way they do. That's a better reason to try it than "it's a classic," and it's the only reason I'd offer.

Rules for picking

Reach for

  • A director already scored 4+ with films left — this is the most reliable predictor on the page
  • Someone splits, doubles, or loses hold of who they are and the film resolves it
  • Horror with a real idea under it. You'll watch bad horror happily but you only rate the thoughtful kind
  • Comedy that's actually cruel: Lebowski, King of Comedy, Triangle of Sadness, Bad Trip, Bugonia
  • Big metaphysical swing with an emotional landing — Prometheus at 4½ over most prestige titles says this outranks execution

Avoid

  • Spectacle or cruelty with no payoff — three misses in one batch (Beau, Sacred Deer, Enemy) all share this
  • Surrealism where decoding is the experience (Mulholland Drive)
  • Competent craft with nothing underneath (The Batman at 2½, the Bourne films)
  • Long and stately. The runtime and performance style are the friction — not the age

What this can't see

  • Only 30 scores across 340 films. The profile knows what you love and almost nothing about what you actively dislike — The Batman is still the only real negative. Twenty more ratings, especially bad ones, would sharpen this more than anything else here.
  • Six films you've confirmed seeing aren't scored yet: Dunkirk, Enemy, Sacred Deer, Beau, Eddington, and the Tarantino back half.
  • Five discussed films aren't logged at all: The Master, Crimes and Misdemeanors, Sweeney Todd, Oddity, plus rewatches.
  • Non-English cinema is thin — Parasite, Oldboy, City of God, Dogtooth, Spirited Away, and the two Bergmans. Not a stated dislike, just unexplored. The two Koreans at the top of Watch next are the cheapest way in.
